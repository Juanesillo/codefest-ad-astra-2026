Focus and Structure
An OEWG is a well-known type of UN process, although it has not been used for space security issues recently. Unlike the GGE
process, which has a fixed number of par(cid:415)cipants who are meant to be independent of their governments, an OEWG is open to
all UN members who would like to par(cid:415)cipate and the delega(cid:415)ons do so in their official capacity. Over the course of the four
mee(cid:415)ngs, more than 70 countries par(cid:415)cipated and 50 made statements, a significant increase over the countries able to
par(cid:415)cipate in the 2011 GGE. In addi(cid:415)on, most of the OEWG process was open to civil society organiza(cid:415)ons, enabling them to
par(cid:415)cipate in what historically had been a states-only process.
The OEWG on Reducing Space Threats had a significant shi(cid:332) in focus from several of the previous space security efforts,
especially PAROS. The OEWG was an a(cid:425)empt to shi(cid:332) the space security discussion from banning or controlling specific
technologies to looking at ac(cid:415)ons and behaviors in space and developing a more unified perspec(cid:415)ve on threats to space
security. It was also designed to be inclusive of both legally-binding measures, such as those championed by Russia and China
with their PPWT, and voluntary norms, which were preferred by the United States and its allies. The discussion within the
OEWG focused on five types of threats:
· Nature and uses of the outer space environment and space systems in rela(cid:415)on to current and future threats by
states to space systems
· Current and future Earth-to-space threats by states to space systems
· Current and future space-to-space threats by states to space systems
· Current and future space-to-Earth threats by states to space systems
· Current and future Earth-to-Earth threats by states to space systems
Results and Next Steps
The four working sessions of the OEWG on Reducing Space Threats featured many substan(cid:415)ve statements by dozens of
countries across a range of space security topics, significantly more so than other recent mul(cid:415)lateral space security discussions,

United States announced a voluntary moratorium on destruc(cid:415)ve tes(cid:415)ng of ground-based ASAT missiles, which was echoed by
34 more countries over the course of the OEWG.12

summary report of their discussions, let alone a set of recommenda(cid:415)ons. This was largely due to blocking by Russia and China,

discussions will con(cid:415)nue. A new GGE on PAROS, championed by Russia and China and focused on their dra(cid:332) PPWT and other
